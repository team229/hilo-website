Why "Just Get a New AC" Is More Complicated Than It Sounds
You'd think buying an AC is like buying an appliance — pick one, plug it in, done. It's not, not even close. A proper installation involves a handful of decisions that genuinely affect how the system performs for the next 15-plus years:
- Correct sizing for your home's square footage, insulation, and layout
- Ductwork condition — leaky or undersized ducts can tank efficiency even with a brand-new unit
- Refrigerant line setup, done to spec so the system charges correctly
- Electrical compatibility, especially in older LA homes that weren't wired with modern HVAC loads in mind
- Placement of the outdoor unit, which affects both efficiency and how much noise you'll hear from your patio
- Permits and code compliance, which some companies skip to save time (and which can bite you later if you sell the house)
Skip or rush any of that, and you'll end up with a system that technically runs but never quite performs the way it should.
The Sizing Mistake That Costs People the Most
If there's one thing that trips up AC installation Los Angeles projects more than anything else, it's sizing. People assume bigger is better — get the biggest unit that'll fit the budget, right? Actually, no. An oversized unit cools the room fast, shuts off, and cycles on and off constantly, which wears out components faster and leaves the air feeling humid and uneven. An undersized one just never keeps up, running nonstop and still not hitting the temperature you want.
Getting the size right takes an actual load calculation — square footage, ceiling height, window placement, insulation quality, even which direction your house faces. It's a bit more work upfront, but it's the difference between a system that runs quietly and efficiently for 15 years and one that's fighting itself the whole time.

Signs It's Actually Time to Replace, Not Repair
Not everyone needs a new system just because one thing broke. A few signs it's genuinely time to consider AC installation Los Angeles style, rather than another repair:
- Your unit is 12 to 15 years old or older
- You're calling for repairs more than once a year
- Your energy bills keep climbing despite regular maintenance
- The system uses older refrigerant that's being phased out and getting harder to source
- A technician has quoted a repair that costs more than 40-50% of a new system
If two or more of those sound familiar, it's worth at least getting a quote before sinking more money into an aging unit.
