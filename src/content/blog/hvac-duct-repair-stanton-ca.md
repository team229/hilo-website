Quick answer: HVAC duct repair fixes leaks, loose joints, crushed sections and damaged insulation in the ducts that carry air through your home. Consider it when rooms heat or cool unevenly, energy bills rise for no clear reason, or you hear whistling at the vents. Minor damage often needs sealing or patching. Severely damaged or poorly sized ducts may need replacement. A professional inspection tells you which path fits.

## Why Does Duct Repair Matter for Your Home?

Ducts move air from your system to every room. When they leak, conditioned air escapes before it reaches you. The U.S. EPA's ENERGY STAR program states that in a typical house, about 20 to 30 percent of the air moving through the duct system is lost to leaks, holes and poorly connected ducts.

That loss shows up as higher bills, weak airflow and rooms that never feel right. Many homeowners blame the air conditioner when the real problem sits in the attic.

## What Is HVAC Duct Repair?

HVAC duct repair is the process of finding and fixing damage in a duct system without replacing the whole thing. Technicians seal leaks, reconnect separated sections, patch holes and restore insulation. You may also see it called ductwork repair, air duct repair or leaky duct repair.

Residential duct repair differs from duct cleaning. Cleaning removes debris. Repair fixes the physical damage that wastes air. See HiLo Heating and Air's [duct cleaning service](/services/indoor-air-quality-solutions/duct-cleaning/) if debris is also a concern.

## How Do You Know You Need Duct Repair?

Look for these signs:

- One room stays warm while another feels fine.
- Energy bills climb without a change in habits.
- Vents whistle or rattle.
- Dust builds up quickly around the home.
- Airflow feels weak even though the system runs.
- You see crushed, sagging or disconnected ducts in the attic.

Ducts fail gradually. A seam loosens, a section gets crushed, or rodents chew through insulation. Uneven rooms sometimes call for [air balancing](/services/indoor-air-quality-solutions/hvac-air-balancing/) after repairs, so ask whether it applies to your home.

## Should You Repair or Replace Your Ducts?

Repair works when damage is limited and the ducts are in fair condition. Replacement is the better call when ducts are brittle, undersized, badly damaged or mismatched to your current equipment.

Here is a simple guide:

1. Small gaps at seams: [Duct sealing](/services/indoor-air-quality-solutions/hvac-duct-sealing/) may be enough.
2. Localized damage: Patch, reconnect or replace the affected section.
3. Old, brittle or collapsing ducts: Plan for HVAC duct replacement or ductwork replacement.
4. Wrong size for the system: [Duct installation](/services/indoor-air-quality-solutions/duct-installation/) with correct sizing may be necessary. See the duct installation page.

Patching ducts that are failing everywhere only delays the real fix. A good contractor shows you what they found and explains why.

## What Does a Proper Duct Repair Involve?

A proper job starts with inspection. HiLo Heating and Air states that it checks accessible ductwork and uses smoke or pressure testing where needed to find leaks. The next steps depend on what the inspection shows.

Quality matters at the sealing stage. The U.S. Department of Energy says duct mastic is the preferred material for sealing seams and joints and warns that cloth-backed rubber adhesive duct tape tends to fail quickly. ENERGY STAR also advises against ordinary duct tape. HiLo says it seals with mastic, not tape.

- How do you find leaks? Smoke or pressure testing beats a quick look.
- What sealing material do you use? Mastic or approved metal tape, not household duct tape.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Using duct tape on seams.
- Repairing only visible leaks and ignoring return ducts.
- Replacing ducts when sealing would do.
- Repairing ducts that are too damaged to hold.
- Skipping insulation on ducts in hot attics.
- Ignoring recurring pest damage.

Professional help makes sense for ducts in attics and crawlspaces. The Department of Energy notes that minor repairs are easy, but qualified professionals should seal and insulate ducts in unconditioned spaces.
